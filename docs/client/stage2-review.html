What the demo shows

The attached video records the real player and the real broadcast on the test server, including the audio. Each test mix has a voice saying "minute N" once a minute, so you can hear where in the mix the station is.

Key point: this is radio, not a playlist

When a listener presses Play, they join the station at its current point. In the demo, playback is paused at 2:17 into a mix and resumed about a minute and a half later at 3:56, because the station kept broadcasting while nobody was listening. Stations run 24/7 whether or not anyone is tuned in.

Decisions needed to start Stage 3

  1. Approve the approach. AzuraCast for broadcasting and admin, a small station list service for the game, and outside stations such as OnTop FM played directly from their own stream.
  2. Approve hosting. OVH UK server, about £17 a month plus VAT, with unlimited traffic.
  3. Domain names. For example radio.yourdomain.com, used for the station list and streams.
  4. Adverts within long mixes. Confirm that splitting mixes into roughly 30-minute parts is acceptable, or say if adverts should only play between whole mixes.
  5. OnTop FM. Stream address and written permission from the owner.
  6. Content. First real mixes, adverts and station artwork.
  7. Licensing. Contact PPL and PRS for Music before public launch. The prepared service description explains how the radio works (in-game, after login, advert-supported, five stations, long repeat cycles).
  8. iPhone volume. Accept that on iPhone, volume is controlled with the phone's buttons. This is an Apple restriction that applies to every web player.

Worth knowing

After a station is restarted, AzuraCast starts the next mix from its beginning rather than resuming the interrupted one. Normal 24/7 running is unaffected.

Licensing bodies usually want reports of the individual tracks played. Since stations play whole DJ mixes, tracklists from the DJs will be needed for that reporting.
